About the Agent-Facts Protocol • Last updated: June 7, 2026

Agent-Facts is an open standard for publishing a structured, machine-readable file of business facts that AI agents can retrieve and trust. The current version is v3.0, published April 15, 2026.

The problem it solves

AI systems routinely state business details that are wrong: outdated prices, hours that no longer apply, services a business does not offer, even a fabricated founder name. They do this because there is no reliable, structured source of truth for them to read, so they infer from scraped pages and stale training data. Agent-Facts gives a business one canonical file, hosted on its own domain, that answers the questions agents actually ask and records where each fact came from and when it was last verified.

What it is, and what it is not

Agent-Facts is the structured facts layer that an llms.txt file can point to. It is a static, zero-dependency data file any AI agent can parse today: the "what we do, what we charge, and what we explicitly do not do" source that prevents hallucination.

It is not a replacement for llms.txt, MCP, NLWeb, or WebMCP. It is not a universal verification or identity protocol, not a cryptographic trust chain, and not an AI-agent identity or governance framework. Other projects using similar names address those areas. Agent-Facts is strictly about business facts for AI retrieval.

History

v2.1 (early 2026)
The first published draft, distributed as a PDF specification. It established the core ideas: a clean, recency-stamped facts page and a mandatory section listing what a business does not do.
v2.2 (March 2, 2026)
Added stricter timestamp rules, provenance labels, a set of core required fields, anti-spam directives, and cross-file consistency requirements. A backward-compatible refinement ahead of the larger v3.0 overhaul.
v3.0 (April 15, 2026)
Introduced agent-facts.json as the canonical machine-readable format, with agent-facts.html as its human-readable projection. Added fact-level provenance and freshness, the twelve Golden Questions, three conformance levels (Minimal, Recommended, Full), and an adoption roadmap.

How the specification is developed

Agent-Facts v3.0 was developed and refined with input from multiple leading AI systems: Perplexity, Grok, Anthropic (Claude), Google (Gemini), OpenAI (ChatGPT), and Microsoft Copilot. The final v3.0 revision was prepared with Claude Opus 4.6. This multi-model approach is intentional: a protocol meant to be read by many different language models is more robust when those same models help shape how it is written and tested.

Status and governance

The protocol is a Public Draft (Pre-RFC) and is open for implementation by anyone. The specification is free to use with no license fees and no registration. Proposed changes are reviewed on a quarterly cycle. Suggestions and real-world implementation feedback are welcome by email.

Maintainer

The protocol is maintained by Ian Aguirre, founder of AgentReadyScan.com, based in Chicago, Illinois. The specification itself is independent and free. AgentReadyScan provides separate, optional tooling that helps businesses implement the protocol; the protocol does not require it.
